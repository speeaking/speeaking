# Investigación legal: contenido de usuarios, derechos de autor y marketplace (México)

Fecha: 2026-10-07. Hecha con agentes de investigación sobre fuentes primarias (DOF, Cámara de Diputados, SCJN, SAT, PROFECO, IMPI, INDAUTOR) y verificada hallazgo por hallazgo por un segundo agente. **No es asesoría legal**: es el insumo para la revisión de un abogado mexicano. Los hallazgos marcados «descartado» se omiten; los «corregido» ya traen la corrección. El texto de los hallazgos está en inglés (como salió de la investigación); los resúmenes de decisión, en español. Lo que se implementó está en ADR-076 (docs/decisions.md).

## Decisiones que tocan al fundador

- Who the responsable and operator is (persona física with business activity, or incorporate an SAS before real charges), and a publishable domicile that can receive legal notifications (fiscal domicile or a virtual office, not your home), RFC and phone.
- Create and monitor the mailboxes: derechos@ (with an alternate address for notices), privacidad@, soporte@ and legal@ or autoridades@. Who answers each, and their SLAs: acknowledgement within 1 business day; removal within 24-48 h of a complete notice; 24 h or less for intimate content and content involving minors.
- Strike policy: 3 strikes in 12 months closes the account; immediate closure for piracy-dedicated stores; whether the policy also covers social posts and linked accounts (recommended: yes).
- Trademark notices: a short Mercado Libre-style exchange (pause, store replies within N business days, holder silence means reinstatement) or the same 15-business-day logic as copyright; require an IMPI number.
- Whether to register a US DMCA designated agent ($6, renew every 3 years), given hosting on Vercel and Cloudflare R2.
- Music: keep video uploads with audio and no licence (rely on notice-and-takedown), or mute or restrict promoted store videos until a licence exists.
- Do not enable a real Pruébatelo image model until the garment denylist, nudity classifier and visible AI mark are built (recommended).
- Pilot bans not required by law: alcohol, live animals, CBD, adult toys, defence sprays and stun devices, veterinary medicines, realistic replica guns.
- Age policy: 18+ only (recommended) or 13-17 with parental consent.
- Rename «Colaboración» to «Publicidad · Colaboración»; whether «Aparecer en sugerencias» stays on by default.
- When to remove the «Borrador para revisión legal» banner: only on the lawyer-approved version. Meanwhile, publishing the notice channel and the repeat-infringer policy is urgent, because the safe-harbor conditions apply today.
- Go or no-go for real Stripe top-ups: needs an accountant's opinion on the LIVA 18-J trigger, a PAC for CFDI, and the entity and RFC.
- Retention periods to publish and enforce: moderation history, notice case files, activity logs, sanction history (at most 72 months).
- Whether memes or parody of third-party works are allowed as a policy, knowing Mexico has no clear exception for them and they come down when the holder claims them.

## Derechos de autor y responsabilidad de la plataforma

speeaking qualifies as a «Proveedor de Servicios en Línea» (LFDA art. 114 Septies fr. II b and c). It is shielded from copyright damages for user content only while it meets every condition of art. 114 Octies: it must not control, initiate or direct the infringement; it must remove content quickly («de manera expedita y eficaz») once it has a valid notice or an authority order; it must take reasonable steps to stop the same content being uploaded again; it must notify users when it removes content on its own initiative; it must have a public policy for closing repeat infringers' accounts; it must not interfere with standard technical measures; and it must not take a financial benefit directly attributable to infringement it can control. The biggest new development is the decree published in the DOF on 24-09-2026, which amends the LFDA Regulation (new arts. 37 Bis–37 Nonies). It sets the required contents of notices and counter-notices and requires a clear, accessible form. Platforms may not demand registration certificates or other documents from people who send notices. Removed content must be restored 10–15 business days after a counter-notice unless the notifier proves it started legal proceedings. The decree takes effect 90 business days after publication (about early-to-mid February 2027; confirm). Failing to remove content after a notice is also a separate fine handled by IMPI, not only a loss of the safe harbor (art. 232 Quinquies: 1,000–20,000 UMA, about MXN 117k–2.35M). The live /terminos page has no formal notice channel, no counter-notice procedure and no repeat-infringer policy, and the draft in docs/legal/02 still requires documents that the new Regulation forbids. The safe harbor conditions apply today, so this is the most urgent gap. The SCJN upheld the whole 2020 regime on 3 June 2024 (AI 217/2020 and 249/2020) and invalidated nothing. Six justices voted to invalidate the voluntary good-faith removal clause, but 8 votes are needed, so the challenge was dismissed («desestimada»). The 14-05-2026 reform narrowed art. 87 so that only performers' image and voice are protected, and that protection explicitly covers AI outputs. It also banned AI voice clones of performers (art. 118 fr. VII), and breaking either rule for profit is now an IMPI fine (art. 231 fr. II). Content generated only by AI has no copyright (art. 12; SCJN AD 6/2025). speeaking's own AI features (Pruébatelo, stylist looks, editorial posts) are probably outside the safe harbor and need their own rights clearance. The other practical risks are sellers copying product photos, commercial music in store videos (speeaking has no music licence), the image pipeline stripping copyright metadata, and indigenous designs on fashion items under the 2022 cultural-heritage law.

## Protección al consumidor y comercio electrónico

On 2026-10-07 the Cámara de Diputados compilation still shows the last LFPC reform as DOF 12-12-2025. That reform added art. 76 BIS fr. VIII–IX on recurring charges and immediate cancellation, effective the next day. The 2026 fine amounts come from Acuerdo DOF 23-12-2025. Two later LFPC reforms passed the Chamber of Deputies but are still in the Senate (arts. 13/16 on marketing data; cross-border e-commerce). There are also influencer and edited-image initiatives that have not passed. No mandatory e-commerce NOM exists: NMX-COE-001-SCFI-2018 is still in force, and LFPC art. 76 BIS 1 tells online providers to be guided by it. Under that standard speeaking is a «proveedor intermediario» (intermediary provider). Its terms must name the operator: legal name, RFC, physical address in Mexico, phone. They must also split duties with the stores, give a free complaint channel and explain the consumer's rights. The published /terminos page does none of this. It also still shows a «Borrador» (draft) banner, while the much fuller draft in docs/legal/02 is not live. The biggest risks are these. (1) No provider identity or contact before the transactions that are about to start: art. 76 BIS fr. III, fines up to $4.27M MXN. (2) Clauses that may be void under art. 90: changes and suspension without an exit or review; the «Pruébatelo» line that it «no da derecho a devolución» (gives no right to a return); and the /precios note that per-try-on prices can go up with only a notice on that page. (3) The «Colaboración» label. PROFECO's 2023 influencer guide prefers «Publicidad»-type tags and warns against vague words such as «Socio» or «Embajador», so the label is open to challenge under art. 32. (4) Evidence: SCJN jurisprudence 1a./J. 112/2025 says conditions bind a consumer only if they were visible at the moment of purchase. So each order must save a snapshot of the store's conditions, and each acceptance must be kept for 10 years (CCom art. 49; consider NOM-151). With payments still simulated, the 5-business-day cancellation right (art. 56), the 90-day minimum warranty (art. 77) and bonificaciones (refund or replacement plus at least 20%, arts. 92/92 TER) do not apply to real sales yet. But listings, ads and AI drafts are public now, so arts. 7 BIS, 32 and 76 BIS apply today. The Stripe top-ups for stores trigger the identity, total-price-with-IVA, receipt, invoicing and refund duties as soon as they go live, and fr. VIII–IX if auto-recharge is ever offered.

## Impuestos, pagos y regulación financiera

I read the current official texts on 2026-10-07: LIVA (last reform DOF 12-11-2021), LISR (DOF 01-04-2024), LIF 2026 (DOF 07-11-2025), CFF (DOF 09-04-2026), RMF 2026 (DOF 28-12-2025) and its Anexo 7 (DOF 09-01-2026), Ley Fintech (DOF 14-11-2025), LFPIORPI (DOF 16-07-2025) and its Reglamento (DOF 27-03-2026), LIC, LGOAAC and LFPC (DOF 12-12-2025). speeaking is not covered by the Capítulo III Bis rules for foreign digital-service providers. As a Mexican resident it falls under LIVA 1o.-A BIS/18-J once it charges a «contraprestación» for intermediation. Today listing is free and all payments are simulated, so the argument that the regime does not apply yet is strong. When stores start paying real money for «Producto destacado» and «Ver cómo me veo», the SAT can treat those fees as payment for intermediation. That would trigger three things: showing «IVA incluido» on listings, a monthly informative return due by the 10th (seller RFC, CURP, domicilio fiscal and CLABE, filed even if speeaking never collects the price), and real-time SAT access under CFF 30-B (in force since 2026-04-01). Missing the real-time access can lead to the service being blocked. ISR/IVA withholding (ISR 2.5% under LIF 2026, IVA 50%, 20%/100% without RFC) applies only once speeaking collects the sale price on the sellers' behalf. The store saldo can fall under the Ley Fintech art. 24 fr. II exclusion for prepayments that only the issuer accepts and that cannot be converted to money, so no IFPE license is needed. This holds only while the saldo is never transferable, never withdrawable and never accepted by sellers. Unauthorized FPE activity carries 7–15 years of prison. Top-ups of $99–$799 are far below the LFPIORPI 645 UMA threshold (about $75,665 MXN in 2026), but a cap on cumulative top-ups per store over 6 months is prudent. Two other risks: CFF 113 Bis (reformed DOF 07-11-2025) punishes digital platforms that allow ads selling fake or simulated CFDI («venta de facturas») with 2–9 years, which calls for a specific moderation rule now. And once real buyer payments flow, collecting them in speeaking's own bank account and paying sellers later raises money-transmission, FPE and captación risks, so payments should run through Stripe Connect connected accounts. Correct ADR-044's «CFDI a quien lo pida»: every peso of income needs a CFDI, either individual or a global CFDI to «público en general».

## Imagen, IA, contenido dañino y productos prohibidos

Two legal changes reshape this area. First, the LFDA reform published in the DOF on 2026-05-14 rewrote art. 87. It now protects only the image and voice of performers (artistas intérpretes o ejecutantes), including AI outputs, and no longer protects the "retrato" of an ordinary person. The task brief and the general framing in docs/legal are out of date on this point. For ordinary users, protection of their image now comes from civil law: CCF art. 1916 fr. IV and the CDMX Ley de Responsabilidad Civil para la Protección del Derecho a la Vida Privada, el Honor y la Propia Imagen. Under arts. 18-19 and 26 of the CDMX law, publishing or commercially using someone's image without express consent is unlawful. Second, "Ley Olimpia" already covers simulated intimate content: LGAMVLV art. 20 Quáter says "reales o simulados", which reaches AI deepfakes. Art. 20 Sexies lets the Ministerio Público (MP) or a judge order platforms to block or delete such content, and the platform must tell the uploader at once. A federal bill that names AI-generated intimate images in CPF art. 199 Octies (4–8 years) is in Senate committees, and Jalisco already has its own law. Mexico has no general platform-liability statute, no binding AI-labeling rule and no statutory minimum age for social networks; the age proposals are only initiatives. The main platform risks: «Pruébatelo» makes photorealistic images of real people that can be shared (deepfakes of third parties, minors or sexualized images); the live /terminos page lacks the public repeat-infringer policy and notice channel that the LFDA 114 Octies safe harbor requires; sign-up has no age declaration; the prohibited-goods filter runs only on AI-assisted listings, not manual ones; and there are no report reasons for intimate images, child safety, image misuse or hate speech. Several prohibitions apply directly to internet sales or ads: LFAFE art. 52 (DOF 29-05-2025) bans selling arms, ammunition, accessories, parts and components "a través de las plataformas de Internet"; LGS arts. 282 Quater and 456 Bis (DOF 15-01-2026) make any vape sale or digital advertising a crime of 1–8 years; LGCT art. 16 fr. IV bans selling tobacco online; and trafficking-law art. 33 punishes anyone who runs or edits an electronic medium that publishes content facilitating trafficking with 5–15 years. Civil exposure for leaving defamatory content up after notice is real: Mexico City courts held Google liable over a Blogger blog (Richter case), and the outcome at the Supreme Court (SCJN) is not confirmed.

| Image/voice of artists and AI clones (LFDA 2026 reform)                                        | Since DOF 14-05-2026, LFDA art. 87 covers only the image, including the voice, of performers (personas artistas intérpretes o ejecutantes) and their characters. It may be used or published only with their express consent, and that protection 'abarca los resultados generados por sistemas de inteligencia artificial'. A paid authorization covers only the agreed uses. No consent is needed when the performer is a minor part of a group, for images taken in a public place, for news use, or for parody, satire or creative imitation that is not a misleading clone and does not replace the artist in the market. Art. 118 fr. VII adds a performer's right to oppose AI impersonation and identifiable voice simulation. Art. 121: any AI clone or simulation of a performer's voice or image needs a prior written agreement. Art. 231 fr. II makes breaches of arts. 87 and 118 fr. VII, done for direct or indirect profit, a commercial infringement. The former general rule on the 'retrato de una persona' was removed (secondary source: Expansión quotes Prof. De la Parra, UNAM). — Corrección: Core rule confirmed. Four fixes. (1) The art. 121 paragraph requiring a 'prior written agreement' for AI clones sits in the article on contracts between a performer and an audiovisual producer and speaks of an agreement 'entre las partes'. It is not a general rule binding any third party. For third parties, the operative rules are art. 87 (express consent) and art. 118 fr. VII (right to authorize or prohibit AI impersonation). (2) Art. 87 protects only 'artistas intérpretes o ejecutantes' as defined in art. 116 (actor, narrator, announcer, commercial announcer, dubbing actor, singer, musician, dancer...; extras and occasional participants are excluded). Influencers are covered only if they qualify as performers. Banning all influencer or public-figure look-alikes is policy, not an LFDA duty. (3) The 'unclear' safe-harbor point is now resolved. Reglamento LFDA art. 37 Ter fr. VI (DOF 24-09-2026, in force 90 business days after publication, about early Feb 2027) defines 'material/contenido' for arts. 114 Septies, 114 Octies and 232 Quinquies to include 'la imagen, incluida la voz, de las personas artistas intérpretes o ejecutantes a que se refiere el artículo 87'. Art. 87 claims therefore run through notice-and-takedown. A platform that does not remove expeditiously after a valid notice can itself be fined 1,000–20,000 UMA (art. 232 Quinquies fr. II). (4) The fine in 'días de salario mínimo' (art. 232 fr. I) is read as UMA after the 2016 de-indexation.                                                                                                                                                            | LFDA arts. 87, 118 fr. ## Cómo lo hacen otras plataformas en México

The large platforms selling into Mexico share one core set of clauses: a user-content licence, a not-the-seller disclaimer, an IP notice/appeal channel, a repeat-infringer rule, and a carve-out keeping Mexican consumer law. Their details differ widely. Mercado Libre (terms version of 30-09-2026), Amazon MX and Shein MX choose Mexican law and keep consumer rights, and Amazon names PROFECO outright. Meta does the same for consumers, letting them sue at home. The base TikTok terms point to Singapore arbitration, which LFPC art. 90 VI makes void against Mexican consumers. Only TikTok Shop's sales and logistics clauses send disputes to Mexican law and federal courts in Mexico City. For copyright, Mexico has a statutory notice-and-takedown safe harbour (LFDA art. 114 Octies, added 2020; the Supreme Court (SCJN) upheld it on 3-Jun-2024). It requires expedited removal, steps against re-upload, notice to the uploader, a counter-notice with a 15-business-day window, and a public repeat-infringer policy. speeaking's draft already copies this, but the product has no notice form, strike counter, moderation notice or appeal button yet, so the safe harbour is not reachable today. Three gaps stand out against the benchmarks. (1) Formal IP notices share the rights holder's name and contact with the uploader, as Meta, TikTok and TikTok Shop do; speeaking's live terms promise reporters anonymity without separating community reports from formal notices. (2) speeaking has no explicit seller licence covering AI-derived uses of product photos (Pruébatelo, stylist, collaborations); Mercado Libre's licence expressly covers AI editing. (3) There is no rule or auto-label for AI-generated likenesses. TikTok bans AI likenesses of private adults used without permission, and the LFDA reform published in the DOF on 14-05-2026 now extends performers' image and voice protection to AI-generated results (art. 87) and requires prior written agreement for AI voice/image cloning (art. 121). Avoid copying the perpetual, irrevocable licences used by TikTok, Amazon and Shein: LFDA arts. 30-33 require licences in writing and transfers to be paid and time-limited, so Meta's model (licence ends when the content is deleted, which speeaking's A10 already follows) is the safer one. Mercado Libre's Brand Protection Program and TikTok Shop's IPPC and points system (0-1000 score, points expire after 180 days, at most two appeals within 30 and 15 days) give a ready design for brand protection and strikes. Etsy pages blocked automated access, so they were not used; TikTok Shop seller document requirements were confirmed only through a secondary source.
